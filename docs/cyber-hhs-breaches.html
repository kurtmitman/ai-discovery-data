Breaches of 500+ people

The final bar is a partial quarter.

Definition

The HHS Office for Civil Rights publishes reported breaches of unsecured protected health information that affected 500 or more people on its breach portal [@hhs2026breachportal]. The portal splits them into two listings:

This page lists all HIPAA breaches reported within the last 24 months that are currently under investigation by the Office for Civil Rights. — HHS Office for Civil Rights, Breach Portal, current listing, read 2026-11-03 [@hhs2026breachportal]

This page archives all resolved HIPAA breach reports and/or reports older than 24 months. — HHS Office for Civil Rights, Breach Portal, archive listing, read 2026-11-03 [@hhs2026breachportal]

A counted event is one listed breach, dated by its breach submission date. The split uses the portal's "Type of Breach" field: a breach whose type includes "Hacking/IT Incident" is counted there, and every other type (unauthorized access or disclosure, theft, loss, improper disposal, other) is counted together. The people-affected figures are the "Individuals Affected" the reporting entity stated.

Method

fetch.py reads both listings from the portal. The portal is a JavaServer Faces application with no plain export URL, so the script replays the steps a browser takes: it opens the report page through the front page's "View HIPAA Breach Reports" link, submits the listing's "Export as CSV" control, switches to the archive tab and exports again. Controls are found by their labels rather than by their generated identifiers. The script stops if a column header moves, if either export is empty, or if any breach appears in both listings. It keeps every column but the free-text web description, drops breaches submitted after the snapshot date, and writes the ledger with a listing column recording which listing each row came from, then the quarterly and annual rollups.

figure.py draws the quarterly counts with the shared periodic_stacked() shape, Hacking/IT Incident under every other type, in the dark and light ends of the collection's severity ramp: the split is a harm category, and no breach report names who found the flaw. A second chart draws people affected per year in millions with the same split, and its note names every single breach that affected 50 million people or more. The axes are linear, and January 2026 onward is shaded, as in every figure here. check.py recomputes the fact lines above and checks that the rollups sum to the ledger.

Limitations

  • only breaches of 500 or more people are listed. Smaller breaches do not appear on the portal.
  • only HIPAA-covered health data is in scope. The series says nothing about breaches outside health care or of other kinds of data.
  • dates are submission dates. A breach is dated when it was reported to HHS, which can follow the breach itself by months, and the listing lags submissions, so the latest weeks are incomplete.
  • "Hacking/IT Incident" is not "exploited vulnerability". The category includes ransomware, phishing that compromised an account, and network intrusions; the portal does not record the access method or any CVE.
  • people affected is the reporting entity's own count. It can be revised after submission, and one large breach can dominate a year.
  • rows are reports, not incidents. The ledger keeps the listing's rows as they are and does not merge rows that may describe one incident, such as a business associate's breach listed under several covered entities.

AI attribution

The portal records breach type and location, not who carried out a breach or how. No AI attribution appears in either listing as of the 2026-11-03 read [@hhs2026breachportal].
