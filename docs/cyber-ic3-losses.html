Definition

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) takes complaints from victims of internet-enabled crime and publishes an annual Internet Crime Report [@fbi2026ic3]. Each report tabulates complaints and reported losses by crime type, with ransomware as one row.

The counted event here is one complaint identified as ransomware in the year of the report. The two dollar columns are what complainants reported losing: ransomware losses from the crime-type loss table, and total reported losses across every crime type from the report's own headline figure. Neither is an estimate of the cost of ransomware or of cybercrime; the reports say what the ransomware figure leaves out:

Regarding ransomware adjusted losses, this number does not include estimates of lost business, time, wages, files, or equipment, or any third-party remediation services acquired by an entity. In some cases, entities do not report any loss amount to FBI, thereby creating an artificially low overall ransomware loss rate. Lastly, the number only represents what entities report to FBI via IC3 and does not account for the entity directly reporting to FBI field offices/agents. — FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, 2024 Internet Crime Report, footnote to the crime-type loss table, read 2026-11-03 [@fbi2026ic3]

Total reported losses span every crime type the reports tabulate, most of them frauds that involve no software flaw. The tables record neither how a ransomware attacker gained access nor which vulnerability, if any, was used.

Method

There is no fetch.py in this folder. Each row is transcribed by hand from that year's report, read as a PDF from the upstream listing. The ransomware columns come from the row labelled Ransomware in the report's two crime-type tables, by complaint count and by complaint loss. The total-loss column comes from the report's headline statement for its own year, and the losses_as_stated column keeps that statement's wording verbatim; for 2015 it is the totals row of the victims-by-age table, the only place that report states an exact annual total. source and source_url name the report for every row. check.py recomputes the fact lines above and checks that each losses_usd value is the figure its losses_as_stated text states.

Later reports restate earlier years in five-year comparison tables, sometimes with different rounding (the 2022 report's five-year chart puts 2020 at $4.2 billion where the 2020 report says "exceeding $4.1 billion"). Each row here uses its own year's report only.

figure.py draws the ransomware complaints as one bar per year in the darkest severity-ramp colour, and the total reported losses as one grey bar per year in billions of dollars; the grey marks the totals as the agency's own figures, most rounded to the nearest $0.1 billion. The axes are linear, and January 2026 onward is shaded, as in every figure here, though no row reaches it.

Limitations

  • complaints are a sample, not a census. A complaint exists only when a victim files one with IC3; the quoted footnote states that reports made directly to FBI field offices are not counted.
  • the loss columns understate cost. Ransomware losses exclude lost business, recovery and remediation, per the quoted footnote, and many complaints report no loss.
  • the total is not a vulnerability measure. Reported losses span every crime type; the folder keeps them as scale for the ransomware figures, not as a harm attributable to software flaws.
  • the ransomware row counts complaints, not attacks. Nothing in the tables links a complaint to the vulnerability or access method used.
  • the rounding differs by year. 2015's total is exact to the dollar; most later totals are stated to $0.1 billion and several as "exceeding" a rounded figure.
  • the series is annual and lags. The report for a year appears in the spring after it, so no 2026 value exists at this read.

AI attribution

The crime-type tables record neither the attacker's method nor its tooling. No AI attribution appears in the ransomware rows of the 2015–2025 reports as of the 2026-11-03 read [@fbi2026ic3].
